AI denotes computer systems that imitate human intelligence in particular activities. In medical imaging, AI algorithms are trained on huge medical image datasets (X-rays, CT scans, MRIs) and related diagnoses to identify patterns and delicate internal anomalies in the images. Thus, it assists healthcare professionals in improving accuracy since AI analyzes immense amounts of data with influential details and detects slight indications of illness that can be unobserved by the human eye. AI is a reliable "second-reader" that provides systematic analysis and reduces the risk of missed diagnoses. Furthermore, AI helps adapt treatment plans based on particular characteristics identified in the patient's imaging scans. This customized approach leads to effective treatments with possibly fewer side effects.
However, its effective implementation necessitates attentive attention to practical and ethical issues. Ensuring that AI-powered health innovations are available worldwide without provoking existing inequalities in health is a major challenge. The accuracy of AI algorithms relies on the representativeness and quality of the trained data. Thus, biases in the trained data cause biased AI outputs.
